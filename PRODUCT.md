# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The project serves two related audiences.

The Pine Tree Analytics homepage speaks to broader analytics buyers: local businesses, operators, and prospective clients who need clearer reporting, decision support, reputation intelligence, or analyst-led systems without buying a generic software product.

The GuestSignal service page speaks primarily to hospitality buyers: Portland-area independent restaurant, bar, and hospitality owner-operators who are time-poor, reputation-aware, and evaluating whether an outside analyst can help them respond to reviews and understand guest feedback.

Future surfaces may include product and report UI, including interactive reputation reports, approval flows, dashboards, and operational review tools. Those should be treated as product-register surfaces when they are the primary object of work.

## Product Purpose

Pine Tree Analytics presents a Portland-based, analyst-led service business that turns messy public feedback and business data into clearer decisions.

GuestSignal is the flagship service: reputation intelligence for independent restaurants and hospitality businesses. It helps clients keep their public review presence active, respond in their own voice, identify guest-experience patterns, and decide what to do next.

Success means the homepage feels credible for broader analytics work while the GuestSignal page feels specific, local, and immediately useful to hospitality owners. The experience should convert interest into a Review Rescue Audit or sample report request.

## Brand Personality

Calm local expert. Practical, warm, precise, and quietly premium.

The brand should feel like a careful Portland analyst is paying attention to the details owners do not have time to monitor. It should communicate human judgment, local context, and trustworthy execution without sounding corporate, generic, or overproduced.

## Anti-references

Do not make this feel like a SaaS product. Avoid generic dashboard theater, inflated feature grids, fake platform screenshots, startup-style metric hero sections, and language that implies the client is buying software to manage themselves.

Do not make this feel like a generic digital marketing website. Avoid vague growth claims, social media agency language, stock-photo polish, funnel jargon, and broad promises that could fit any local agency.

Do not make this feel like an agency. Avoid large-team positioning, abstract service menus, generic case-study tropes, and brand language that hides the analyst-led nature of the work.

The preferred feel is Portland-based, analyst-led, service-oriented, specific, and human. The site should suggest a serious solo expert with strong systems, not a faceless platform or outsourced marketing shop.

## Design Principles

Show the signal, not the software. Use report previews, review examples, and operational recommendations to make the value concrete without implying the buyer must manage a dashboard.

Lead with practical trust. The design should feel calm, competent, and specific enough for an owner to believe the details will be handled carefully.

Keep local context visible. Portland, independent operators, seasonality, and hospitality realities should shape the tone and examples.

Separate parent and flagship. Pine Tree Analytics should remain broad enough for future analytics work, while GuestSignal should feel focused and highly productized for hospitality.

Human judgment stays central. AI can be mentioned as assistance, but the positioning should emphasize analyst-led work, owner approval, and escalation judgment.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for contrast, keyboard access, focus visibility, and readable type sizing.

Respect reduced-motion preferences and avoid motion that blocks comprehension. Interactive report and product surfaces should remain understandable without animation.

Avoid color-only meaning in charts, report callouts, pricing emphasis, and status indicators. Use labels, text, shape, or hierarchy alongside color.
